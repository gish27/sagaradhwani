# 07 · Editorial & Footage Policy

**Applies to:** everyone who captures, writes, edits, clears or publishes. Signed with the job description (plan §8.2). **Source:** plan §5, §6, §9, §11.

> Working standard, not legal advice. A media lawyer confirms it before launch, especially the amber section (§6).

## 1. Purpose and values

The channel is **not a news channel**. Its intent is to awaken local people through their own place. Daily politics runs alongside because it brings people back every day; identity content is the soul.

**Ethos:** Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam — attaching people to their own land and culture without hostility to anyone. Political and social contest is accepted as part of how a society evolves.

**Values filter (§9.1):** nothing that goes against the nation or against dharma — dharma meaning truthfulness and love for the land, not religion.

**Political position (§9.3, status: open between A and B; operating rule below applies either way):**
- The daily news stays straight. A channel that criticises its own side when warranted is more persuasive, not less.
- The mission lives in identity content, where it cannot be called propaganda.
- **Hard local rule:** going soft at taluk level is not survivable. If a local representative is sitting on a broken road and the channel goes quiet, that is the day trust dies.

## 2. Content layers and daily mix (§4)

| Layer | Holds | Daily count |
|---|---|---|
| Daily pulse | Taluk politics/administration, district developments, state and central news framed by local impact | 2–3 local + 2–3 state/central (filtered) |
| Identity | History, personalities, culture/festivals, livelihood/economy, sports, literature/language | 1 (daily during a festival) |
| Participation | Youth voices, grievances, question of the week, audience stories | as arises |

**Pacing:** run three or four identity buckets weekly; rotate the rest monthly.
**The selection of state stories stays with the Taluk Editor** — it is what makes the channel local rather than a repeater.

## 3. Verification

- Every claim checked: names, figures, spellings, designations.
- Every interview: full name, designation and place spelled out on camera or in the log.
- **One named verifier for all political content** (content head).
- Retold stories: cross-check facts against at least one primary source.
- Unverified viral content is never presented as fact.

## 4. Retelling workflow — sourcing from other reporting (§5)

**Principle:** facts and events are not copyrightable; the specific expression is. Take the underlying story, never the presentation.

| Take | Never take |
|---|---|
| The facts and events | Their script or sentence structure |
| The underlying story | Their footage or photographs |
| Publicly available information | Their narration or voice · their graphics or layout |

**Steps (mandatory, enforced in the script tool):**
1. Pick the source story.
2. Extract **only the facts** as bullet points. Set the original aside.
3. Write the Kannada script **fresh from the facts**, in our own voice and structure.
4. Cross-check against at least one primary source.
5. Add Karnataka or taluk context where the story allows.
6. AI may polish the draft **only after** the writer has decided the structure.

**Never** feed a source script into AI and ask for a translation or rephrase — the result follows the original's order and structure and is still their expression.

**The better answer for state news (§6.5):** not clips at all. Our narration explaining what a state decision means for this taluk — ninety seconds, a still, a graphic or a text card. Unarguably ours.

## 5. Voice (§5.4)

- One AI Kannada voice on all narration; it is channel identity like the logo.
- Interviews and on-camera people remain real human voices.
- Scriptwriters spell proper nouns and numbers phonetically for TTS.

## 6. Footage rules — green / amber / red (§6)

**One-line test:** if someone else's footage *is* our content, that is infringement. If their footage is *what we are talking about*, that is usually defensible.

**When unsure, the piece does not publish until the central desk clears it. No exceptions at taluk level.**

### 6.1 Green — use freely
| Source | Condition |
|---|---|
| Anything our own people shot | Full ownership — the goal for the overwhelming majority |
| Public speeches/rallies we filmed | Ours |
| Government press conferences we filmed | Ours |
| Official government video (PIB, department feeds, state releases) | Generally free with credit; check each portal's licence |
| Parliament and Vidhana Soudha feeds | Public record; credit |
| Material sent by the person in it (MLA's office, college, temple committee, shop) | Sent for publication; keep the message as the record |
| Viewer submissions | Only with written permission in the chat, saved |
| Roster creators | Within standing permission, credited on screen |
| AI visuals for history, culture, ads | Our production — **never for news** |
| Licensed stock and music | Paid or properly free-licensed only |

### 6.2 Amber — only with care and central clearance
Every amber use is short, the source is visibly named, and our own commentary around it is longer than the clip.

| Situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Short extract from another channel's broadcast | Only when the clip is the subject of our story. Brief, their logo visible, credited on screen and in description |
| Viral social video | Verify who shot it; get permission where possible. If unattributable but newsworthy: short portion, credit platform and handle, remove immediately on request |
| A leader's speech carried by a channel we did not film | Prefer the party's own upload or official feed |
| Local creator not on the roster | 15–20 seconds max, credited, our narration doing the main work |
| Photographs from newspapers or websites | Almost never safe. Recreate, license, or shoot our own |
| Film clips, songs, serial footage | Assume no |

### 6.3 Red — never
| Never | Why |
|---|---|
| Another channel's report re-uploaded as our story | Infringement; makes us a repeater |
| Their footage with logo cropped or blurred | Turns a copyright argument into a bad-faith one; fastest route to losing an account |
| Long stretches of another channel's live coverage | No fair-dealing argument survives length |
| Film, serial or music content for entertainment | Guaranteed takedown, possible strike |
| Anyone's private video without consent | Legal exposure; a trust catastrophe in a small taluk |
| CCTV or accident footage of identifiable victims | Dignity; potentially actionable |
| Footage of a minor without a parent's consent | Never |
| **AI-generated visuals inside a news story** | **The hard rule** |
| Unverified viral clips presented as fact | The biggest reputational risk a local channel carries |

### 6.4 Legal ground (§6.4)
Section 52 of the Copyright Act allows fair dealing for reporting current events — narrower than most assume: a short extract, used to comment on the material itself, with credit. It does not protect lifting footage because we could not cover the event. Platforms enforce faster than courts: three strikes ends a channel. With 240 channels under one brand, borrowing is a systemic risk.

### 6.5 Permission checklist — anything we did not shoot (§6.7)
1. Do we know who shot it?
2. Do we have their permission in writing, saved?
3. Is everyone identifiable in it aware it is being published?
4. Any minors? If yes, parent's consent?
5. Have we verified the event happened as claimed, and when?
6. Is the credit on screen?
7. Has the central desk cleared it?

### 6.6 Escalation (§6.6)
- Any amber use goes to the central desk before publishing.
- A takedown notice or rights complaint goes to the central desk the same day; the item comes down immediately while examined.
- Repeated borrowing by a taluk unit is a serious matter, not a style issue.

## 7. AI visuals (§11)

| Allowed | Not allowed |
|---|---|
| Weekly history recreation (one per taluk per week; one consistent visual style) | Anything inside a news story |
| Wider identity content — festivals, traditions, literature | Depicting a real current event as if filmed |
| Advertisements (template and bespoke) | Depicting real people in news contexts |
| Brand package — titles, transitions | — |

AI voiceover on news narration is permitted because it is labelled as the channel's voice, not presented as a real event.

## 8. Escalation before shooting (§8.2)

Anything legally risky, communally sensitive, or involving a serious allegation goes to the state desk **before it is shot**, not after. No taluk unit speaks for the brand to any official, party or publication without approval.

## 9. Paid content and separation (§9.4)

- Paid content is always labelled as paid. A dedicated story about an advertiser is marked as such.
- No coverage is ever promised in exchange for advertising.
- Advertiser screening on "statements against the nation or culture" is a central decision only — at taluk level it can become a tool to block rivals.

## 10. Posters (§5.7)

Quote cards, festival greetings, dates from the taluk's history, event announcements, advertiser creatives. Same rules: our own or AI-made visuals, no borrowed photographs.
