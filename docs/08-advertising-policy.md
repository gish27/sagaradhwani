# 08 · Advertising Policy & Rate Card Structure

**Source:** plan §9.2, §9.4, §10, §11.2, §12.2–12.3. Attached to every rate card so every advertiser sees it upfront.

## 1. How advertising is sold

- **Centrally only.** One sales desk sells taluk, district and state inventory on a fixed, published rate card per tier.
- **Taluk staff have no discounting authority**, cannot promise coverage, and cannot collect cash. This protects them from local pressure.
- **Minimum contract: one month** at taluk level. No daily or single-post sales — collection cost exceeds revenue.
- **Category exclusivity** costs a premium: one jeweller, one hospital, one coaching centre per taluk per month.
- **Bundling discount:** district price < sum of its taluks; state price < sum of its districts.
- **First month free or near-free** in a new taluk: pricing before there is an audience means underpricing permanently or losing the first advertisers. Renewals and names sell the second taluk.
- **Every rupee invoiced and banked.** Payment by bank transfer, UPI or cheque against a GST invoice. **No cash.**
- **Rupee values are blank until the pilot has real audience numbers.**

## 4. Three markets (§10.2)

| Tier | Who buys | Ticket | Sold by | Wants |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Taluk | Shop owner, clinic, coaching centre, dealer — owner decides, one conversation | Small; a few thousand/month; high collection burden | Central desk on fixed card | Footfall from people who know the place |
| District | Dealerships, multi-branch businesses, hospital/school groups, builders — sometimes an agency | Considerably larger; one sale covers many taluks | District sales person | Reach across a catchment |
| State | Brands, agencies, government departments, parties | Large; CPM and reach | Central desk | Reported numbers in a standard format |

**What unlocks agency money (§10.6):** monthly, in a consistent format, per taluk — views, reach, watch time, follower count, age and gender split. Without it only taluk and district tiers are sellable.

## 5. Political and election advertising (§12.3)

| Rule | Implication |
|---|---|
| Regulated by the Election Commission and the Representation of the People Act | Applies to digital channels |
| Political ads on digital media need MCMC pre-certification (district or state) | **No political ad runs uncertified** — certificate reference recorded on the contract |
| Every political ad must disclose who paid | Labelled on screen; "paid for by" recorded |
| 48-hour silence period before polling | No political advertising in that window — system blocks activation |
| Paid news is a serious violation and counts against the candidate's expenditure limit | **Never.** What sinks small local channels |
| Normal period vs election period | Outside the notified period a politician on a podcast is an ordinary sponsored appearance — disclosed as paid. During the notified period, full compliance |

**Structural rule:** with up to 240 taluks, any one can create a violation that lands on the whole brand. All political packages are sold and cleared centrally, never at taluk level.

## 6. Labelling and separation (§9.4)

- Paid content always carries a visible "Paid" / "Sponsored" label.
- A dedicated story about an advertiser is marked as such.
- No coverage is promised or given in exchange for advertising.
- Advertiser creatives follow the footage rules: our own or AI-made visuals, no borrowed photographs.

## 7. AI ad production (§11.2)

Templates by business category — jeweller, clinic, showroom, coaching centre — where name, product and offer are swapped and the ad is finished in under an hour. Bespoke priced at roughly ten times template. Sales weapon: walk into a shop with the ad already made for that shop.

## 8. Collections

- Invoices issued at contract start; monthly in arrears for the first paid month after a free month.
- Collections owner (central) follows up; taluk staff never chase payments.
- Overdue > 30 days pauses the slot; > 60 days lapses the contract.
